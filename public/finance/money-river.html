The Money River

One life, one chart: pocket money at five to an estate settled at the end, with everything in between — income, savings, debt, insurance, investing, a home, a family, and finally what's left to pass on. Eight threads run the whole way; each one thickens, thins, and disappears exactly when the underlying thing does. Every dot is labeled; hover or tap one (or its label) for the story behind it.

How to read it

Age runs left to right, 5 to 90+. Each horizontal band is one thread of financial life — its thickness is roughly "how active or large this is right now," not a precise dollar scale. Bands appear when that thing starts (a first job, a mortgage, a first child) and disappear when it ends (debt paid off, kids independent) or taper together at the dashed line marking death, where whatever's left converges into the estate and forks to the next generation. This traces one representative path — married, a home, two kids, a career of steady raises — not a prediction or a template everyone should match.

Common questions

Frequently asked, in plain language

Why does my life not look like this?

Because this is deliberately one representative path — married with two kids, buys a home, works a steady career. Someone single, renting long-term, self-employed, or without kids would see several bands shift, shrink, or disappear entirely (no mortgage, a differently-timed family band, and so on). The mechanism — bands starting and ending with real events, converging at death — holds regardless of the specific path.

What does a band's thickness actually mean?

Relative intensity or size within that thread, not a precise dollar amount — mortgage debt at its peak is drawn thicker than a starter emergency fund because it's a larger, more active part of that person's finances at that moment, not because of an exact ratio between them.

Where do I go for more depth on any one of these?

The Money Roadmap covers the vocabulary, the priority order, and life-stage detail behind most of these threads. Annuities Explained goes deep on one specific tool that sometimes shows up inside the Investing thread near retirement.

Why does the Estate thread barely exist until the very end?

Legally, it mostly doesn't — a will and beneficiary forms are just paperwork sitting quietly in the background for decades. The thread spikes at death because that's the one moment everything else (remaining cash, investments, home equity, life insurance) actually becomes "the estate" and gets distributed.
